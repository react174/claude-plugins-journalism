# Sports Funding Data Sources

Detailed access guide for each data source used in sports funding research.

---

## Good Jobs First Subsidy Tracker

**URL**: https://subsidytracker.goodjobsfirst.org

**What it contains**: State economic development subsidies awarded to companies, searchable by recipient, state, program, and industry. Includes sports teams and stadium authorities.

**How to search**:
1. Go to subsidytracker.goodjobsfirst.org
2. Use the search bar for team/owner/stadium name
3. Filter by state and/or program type
4. Export results as CSV (button at bottom of results)

**Limitations**:
- Coverage varies by state (some states have poor disclosure laws)
- Primarily state-level; may miss purely local deals
- Not always up to date for recent awards
- Does not include federal programs

**Verification**: Each entry links to a source. Always verify against the linked source document.

## Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB) EMMA

**URL**: https://emma.msrb.org

**What it contains**: Official Statements and continuing disclosures for municipal bonds, including stadium financing bonds issued by cities, counties, and stadium authorities.

**How to search**:
1. Go to emma.msrb.org
2. Search by issuer name (e.g., "St. Louis Convention and Visitors Commission" or "Harris County Sport and Convention")
3. Filter by state and date range
4. Download Official Statements (PDFs)

**What to look for in Official Statements**:
- Total bond amount and term
- Who pays debt service (public entity vs. team)
- Pledge of public revenues (hotel taxes, sales taxes, general fund)
- Projected vs. actual revenue assumptions
- Clawback and default provisions

**Tip**: Stadium authorities often have generic names. Search the city name + "stadium" or "arena" or "convention" or "sport."

## USA Spending

**URL**: https://www.usaspending.gov

**What it contains**: Federal grants, contracts, loans, and direct payments to recipients including sports organizations.

**How to search**:
1. Go to usaspending.gov/search
2. Search recipient name
3. Filter by CFDA program number for specific grant programs
4. Download award details

**Common federal programs for sports**:
- Community Development Block Grants (CDBG): Can fund infrastructure near stadiums
- EDA grants: Economic Development Administration grants for facilities
- HUD programs: Occasionally fund stadium-adjacent development
- Federal historic tax credits: For historic ballpark renovations

## IRS Form 990 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer)

**URL**: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits

**What it contains**: Tax returns (Form 990) for nonprofit organizations, including:
- Professional sports leagues (NFL, PGA Tour, etc. are or were 501(c)(6) nonprofits)
- Conference offices (ACC, SEC, Big Ten)
- University athletic departments (as part of university 990s)
- Stadium and arena authorities organized as nonprofits

**How to search**:
1. Go to projects.propublica.org/nonprofits
2. Search organization name
3. Download available 990 PDFs
4. Key schedules: Schedule O (supplemental info), Part IX (functional expenses), Part VII (executive compensation)

**What to look for**:
- Revenue sources (gate, TV, licensing)
- Related-party transactions (payments to or from team owners)
- Lobbying expenditures
- Executive compensation

## State Budget Portals

Each state publishes budget documents, but formats vary widely.

**General approach**:
1. Search "[state] open budget" or "[state] checkbook" or "[state] transparency portal"
2. Look for line items mentioning the team, stadium, sports authority, or relevant economic development programs
3. Cross-reference with capital budget (infrastructure) and operating budget

**States with notable sports subsidy disclosure**:
- **Ohio**: Ohio Checkbook (ohiocheckbook.com) - searchable expenditures
- **Texas**: Texas Transparency (comptroller.texas.gov/transparency)
- **New York**: OpenBook NY (openbudget.ny.gov)
- **Illinois**: Illinois Comptroller (illinoiscomptroller.gov)
- **California**: State Controller's Office financial data portal

## County Assessor / Property Tax Records

**What it contains**: Property valuations, ownership records, and tax exemption status.

**Why it matters for sports funding**: Stadiums on public land often receive full or partial property tax exemptions. Teams in publicly owned stadiums may pay nothing in property tax.

**How to access**: Most county assessors have online parcel search. Search by:
- Stadium/arena address
- Owner name (often a stadium authority or LLC)
- Parcel number (find from satellite maps or existing documents)

**What to look for**:
- Ownership (public vs. private)
- Assessed value vs. comparable private properties
- Exemption status and category (government-owned, nonprofit, other)
- Payment in lieu of taxes (PILOT) agreements

---

## State and Local Government Audit Reports

**What it contains**: Independent audits of government agencies, including stadium authorities and economic development agencies. Often contain candid assessments of subsidy program effectiveness.

**How to find**:
- State auditor's office website
- Government Accountability Office (GAO) for federal programs
- Local inspector general reports
- Legislative budget office analyses

**Search terms**: "[stadium name] audit" or "[city] stadium authority audit" or "[state] economic development audit"

---

## SEC EDGAR (for public companies)

**URL**: https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar

**When relevant**: When a sports team or its parent company is publicly traded, or when publicly traded companies provide naming rights or other stadium financing.

**What to search**:
- 10-K annual reports: Look for stadium lease terms, subsidy descriptions
- 8-K current reports: Material stadium deal disclosures
- Proxy statements: Related-party transactions between owners and the team

## FOIA Request Targets by Jurisdiction

### Federal FOIA
- **Contact**: Agency-specific FOIA offices
- **Relevant agencies**: HUD, EDA, IRS (for nonprofit status determinations), Treasury (for tax-exempt bond approvals)
- **Submit via**: FOIA.gov or agency-specific portals

### State Records
- Typically filed with the specific agency (state economic development authority, state auditor, governor's office)
- Many states have online portals (e.g., MuckRock.com helps track submissions)

### Local Records
- City/county clerk for ordinances and resolutions approving deals
- City attorney's office for contracts and lease agreements
- Finance department for bond documents and TIF agreements
- Economic development department for subsidy agreements
